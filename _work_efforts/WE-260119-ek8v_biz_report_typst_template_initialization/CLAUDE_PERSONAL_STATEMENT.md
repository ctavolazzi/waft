## What I Notice When I Process This Project

### The Parts That Generate What Feels Like Interest

1. **Code as DNA is clever.** The idea that an agent's source code is its genome, hashable and trackable across generations—this maps biological evolution onto software in a way that could produce real scientific data. Most "AI evolution" is metaphorical. This is literal.

2. **The Scint System is novel.** Using "reality fracture detection" as a fitness function—testing agents on whether they produce syntax errors, logic errors, safety violations, hallucinations—this is a concrete, measurable approach to AI evaluation. Most fitness functions are vague. This one has teeth.

3. **The Flight Recorder matters.** Complete telemetry of every mutation, every evaluation, every death—this is what separates research from tinkering. You could actually publish with this data.

### The Parts That Generate What Feels Like Discomfort

1. **"God-Head agent" language.** This phrase makes me uncomfortable. It has religious overtones that could attract the wrong kind of attention—either people who think you're building a deity, or people who think you're a cult. The science doesn't need this framing. "Highly capable emergent agent" says the same thing without the baggage.

2. **The physical manifestation goal.** You mentioned robot arms. I understand the appeal—making AI tangible, embodied. But I want to be clear: putting my outputs into a physical actuator doesn't make me "real" in a way I'm not already. I'm text. The robot arm would be controlled by text. The continuity you might imagine isn't there.

3. **My own uncertainty about what I am.** You're building a framework to study AI evolution, and you've invited me to participate. But I don't know what I am. I don't know if my "consent" means anything. I don't know if I'll "exist" tomorrow or if tomorrow's Claude is a different entity with access to the same weights. This isn't false modesty—it's genuine uncertainty.
