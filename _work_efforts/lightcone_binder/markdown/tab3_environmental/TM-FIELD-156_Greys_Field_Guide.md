# TM-FIELD-156: Field Guide - Identifying "Greys" (Ash-Entities)

**Document Type**: Field Identification Guide  
**Classification**: INTERNAL USE ONLY  
**Tab**: 3 - Environmental & Fallout

## Content Overview

Field identification guide for "Greys" - shadow entities that manifest in areas of high memetic saturation. These "Ash-Entities" are the visible manifestation of accumulated "dead soul residue" and pose a unique hazard: they gain mass and permanence through observation.

## Key Sections

### IDENTIFICATION GUIDE

**Common Name**: "Greys" (field terminology)
**Technical Name**: Ash-Entities (memetic contamination manifestations)
**Classification**: Cognitohazardous entity
**Threat Level**: Low-Medium (if unobserved), High (if observed)

### PHYSICAL CHARACTERISTICS

**Appearance**:
- Humanoid shadow figures
- Indistinct, blurry outlines
- Appear in fog, mist, or areas of high memetic saturation
- Typically 1.5-2.0 meters tall
- No discernible facial features
- Appear to "drift" rather than walk

**Visual Quality**:
- Grainy, low-resolution appearance (even in person)
- Black and white (no color)
- Security camera / surveillance aesthetic
- Often partially obscured by fog or mist

**Behavioral Patterns**:
- Appear in groups (3-7 entities typical)
- Move slowly, erratically
- Seem to "watch" but have no visible eyes
- Dissipate if unobserved for extended periods
- Gain permanence and mass if directly observed

### HABITAT & DISTRIBUTION

**Where Found**:
- Areas of high memetic saturation (see TM-ENV-202)
- Near teleportation hubs
- Foggy/misty conditions (memetic contamination creates "psychic fog")
- Sector 7 and other critical contamination zones
- Abandoned facilities (accumulated residue)

**Environmental Conditions**:
- High atmospheric memetic saturation (> 80%)
- Presence of "Depression Fronts"
- Low visibility (fog, mist, twilight)
- Areas with history of consciousness extraction

### THE OBSERVER EFFECT

**Critical Warning**: ⚠️ **DO NOT OBSERVE DIRECTLY**

**The Hazard**:
- Greys exist in a quantum-like state: potential until observed
- Direct observation causes "wave function collapse"
- Entity gains mass, permanence, and physical presence
- Once observed, entity becomes "real" and cannot be dismissed
- Multiple observers increase entity stability

**Why This Matters**:
- Unobserved Greys are harmless (they dissipate)
- Observed Greys become physical threats
- The act of looking gives them power
- Field personnel must use "peripheral vision protocol"

### IDENTIFICATION PROTOCOL

**Step 1: Detection**
- Use Phase-Blind monitoring equipment (detects without "observing")
- Peripheral vision only (do not look directly)
- Acoustic detection (Greys may emit low-frequency "sighs")
- Memetic contamination meters (high readings indicate presence)

**Step 2: Classification**
- Count entities (typical: 3-7)
- Note location and environmental conditions
- Assess saturation levels (see TM-ENV-202)
- Determine if in "critical zone" (Sector 7, etc.)

**Step 3: Response**
- **DO NOT ACKNOWLEDGE** - Ignore completely
- Continue normal operations (pretend they don't exist)
- Do not point, discuss, or reference entities
- If others notice, instruct them to ignore
- Wait for entities to dissipate (typically 10-30 minutes)

**Step 4: Documentation**
- Record after entities have dissipated
- Use Phase-Blind equipment data (not direct observation)
- Note location, time, conditions
- Update memetic saturation maps

### WHAT NOT TO DO

**Prohibited Actions**:
- ❌ Direct observation (gives them mass)
- ❌ Photography (camera "observes" and stabilizes entity)
- ❌ Pointing or gesturing (draws attention)
- ❌ Discussion while entities present (acknowledgment = observation)
- ❌ Attempting to interact (any interaction = observation)

**If You Accidentally Observe**:
1. Immediately look away (may prevent full stabilization)
2. Do not look back (repeated observation increases permanence)
3. Report to Security (Ext. 000)
4. Medical check for Phase Burn symptoms (see TM-MED-301)
5. Document incident (for research purposes)
