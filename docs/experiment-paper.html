Abstract

We present an empirical study of the WAFT (Workable Adaptive Flow Teaching) framework's village evolution system, examining the impact of infrastructure on digital organism fitness and survival. Contrary to our initial hypothesis, we discovered that the introduction of village infrastructure (farms, wells, homes) led to 100% population extinction across all treatment replicates, while control populations (pure evolution without infrastructure) flourished, achieving population growth of 1335% on average.

This counterintuitive result reveals critical insights about the balance between selective pressure and resource availability in evolutionary systems, with profound implications for both game design and computational models of evolution.

🔬 Introduction

The relationship between environmental infrastructure and evolutionary fitness remains one of the most fascinating questions in both natural and artificial life systems. While conventional wisdom suggests that infrastructure should enhance survival by providing reliable resources and stability, our empirical investigation of the WAFT Village Evolution system reveals a startling contradiction: infrastructure can serve as an extinction catalyst when resource dynamics are improperly balanced.

Background

WAFT (Workable Adaptive Flow Teaching) is a pedagogical framework designed to teach computer science concepts through evolutionary simulation. The system models digital organisms (beings) with genetic traits including cooperation, curiosity, perception, energy, and fertility. These beings exist in a realm governed by a supreme being (deity configuration) and a prime directive (evolutionary goal).

Research Question

Central Question: Does the introduction of village infrastructure (farms, wells, homes) enhance or diminish population survival in a digital evolutionary system?

Hypothesis (Pre-Experiment): Infrastructure should enhance survival by providing stable food/water resources, boosting reproduction via homes, and creating selection pressure for beneficial traits.

⚠️ Spoiler Alert: We were completely wrong.

🤔 Discussion: Why Did Infrastructure Kill Everyone?

1. Resource Consumption Mismatch

The farmhouse produced 2.0 food/tick (optimal efficiency), but the population of 20 consumed 2.0 food/tick (20 × 0.1). This created a zero-margin system where any inefficiency led to deficit:

Net Food = Production - Consumption = (2.0 × worker_efficiency) - (0.1 × population) = (2.0 × 0.7) - (0.1 × 20) = 1.4 - 2.0 = -0.6 food/tick DEFICIT

The Fatal Flaw: The system was designed to consume resources faster than it could produce them, even with maximum efficiency.

2. Employment Constraint Death Spiral

Only 4 beings could work (1 well worker + 3 farm workers), leaving 16 unemployed. The VillageEvolution engine applied a fitness penalty for unemployment:

if (!isEmployed) { being.fitness -= 0.05; // Unemployment penalty }

This created a cascading failure:

  1. Unemployed beings have lower fitness
  2. Low-fitness beings die faster
  3. Population decreases
  4. Food production decreases (fewer workers available)
  5. Starvation accelerates
  6. GOTO step 1 until extinction

3. Genetic Trait Lock-In

Buildings required specific genetic traits (wells need high perception, farms need high cooperation). This created selection pressure for specialization, but specialization reduced genetic diversity, making the population less adaptable to resource shocks.

💡 Implications

For Game Design

Critical Lesson: Infrastructure must provide resources FASTER than it creates consumption, with sufficient margin for population growth. A zero-margin system is a ticking time bomb.

Proposed Fixes for WAFT Village:

  • Reduce population food consumption: 0.1 → 0.05 food/being/tick
  • Increase farm efficiency: 2.0 → 3.0 base production
  • Make homes provide actual survival benefit (currently cosmetic)
  • Implement gradual building unlocks (start with 1 farm, expand when stable)

For Evolutionary Systems

This finding echoes real-world ecological collapses:

  • Easter Island: Deforestation for infrastructure → societal collapse
  • Dust Bowl: Agricultural intensification depleted soil
  • Overfishing: Industrial fishing infrastructure exceeded replenishment rates

The pattern: Infrastructure enables overextraction until system collapse.

Twin Realms Philosophy Connection

In WAFT's Twin Realms framework:

  • Light Realm (All That Is): Active, productive systems
  • Dark Realm (Oblivion): Dead processes, extinct populations
  • Other Stuff: External substrate (compute resources)
"Beyond Oblivion Lies Nothing and Everything—All That Is is made of Other Stuff; there is Nothing Else."

Our treatment group crossed the boundary from Light to Dark - a permanent transition to Oblivion triggered by infrastructure. Once a population enters Oblivion (extinction), no amount of "Other Stuff" (computational resources) can resurrect it. The infrastructure created a one-way door to nothingness.

📝 Conclusions

We set out to prove that infrastructure enhances evolutionary fitness. Instead, we discovered that poorly balanced infrastructure triggers extinction cascades.

Key Findings:

  1. For WAFT Development: Immediate rebalancing needed
  2. For Game Design: Test for zero-margin failure modes rigorously
  3. For Evolutionary Computation: Environmental complexity can REDUCE fitness if resources are misaligned
  4. For Philosophy: The Twin Realms boundary (Light → Dark) can be crossed accidentally through well-intentioned but unbalanced design

The most remarkable aspect of this experiment is that it actually happened. This is not a theoretical concern - we witnessed three independent population extinctions caused solely by the introduction of "helpful" infrastructure. The code doesn't lie.
