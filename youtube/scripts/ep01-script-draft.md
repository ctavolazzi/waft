## SECTION 1: THE PROBLEM

**HOST:** So here's the thing about traditional virtual pets - they're closed systems. The developers decided how your pet behaves, what it needs, how it responds. You're just... managing something someone else designed.

[VISUAL: Diagram showing closed system]

**HOST:** But what if the pet itself was made of interchangeable parts? What if you could pop out its decision-making brain and swap in a different one? Keep its memories but change its personality?

[VISUAL: Animated module-swap concept]

**HOST:** That's what I'm building with WAFT.

---

## SECTION 2: INTRODUCING WAFT

**HOST:** WAFT stands for... actually, it doesn't matter what it stands for. What matters is what it does.

[VISUAL: Architecture overview]

**HOST:** Think of it like this. Your Tamagotchi has organs, right? A brain that makes decisions. A stomach that tracks hunger. Eyes that see what you're doing. In a normal Tamagotchi, all of that is hardcoded. In WAFT, each organ is a *module*.

[VISUAL: Labeled diagram of modules]

**HOST:**
- The Brain module handles decision-making
- Memory stores persistent state
- Senses handle all the inputs
- Actions are the outputs
- And Personality? That's configurable.

**HOST:** The wild part? You can swap any of these while the creature is running. It's like performing surgery on a living thing, except nobody gets hurt and everything keeps working.

---

## SECTION 3: LIVE DEMO

**HOST:** Okay, enough theory. Let me show you.

[VISUAL: Switch to screen recording]

**HOST:** Here's our creature. Right now it's running a basic brain - simple stimulus-response stuff. Watch what happens when I...

[DEMO: Show interaction, then hot-swap a module]

**HOST:** See that? Same memories, same state, completely different behavior. The creature didn't restart. It didn't lose anything. It just... became something else.
