## Understanding Your Succulent's Needs

### Light Requirements

Most succulents need **bright, indirect light** for 4-6 hours daily. Some varieties can handle direct sunlight, while others prefer filtered light.

<div class="procedure">
<div class="step">
<strong>Observe your plant's location</strong> - Watch for signs of too much or too little light
</div>

<div class="step">
<strong>Rotate regularly</strong> - Turn your succulents weekly to ensure even growth
</div>

<div class="step">
<strong>Adjust seasonally</strong> - Move plants as daylight hours change throughout the year
</div>
</div>

### Watering: The Golden Rule

<div class="warning">
<div class="warning-title">Most Common Mistake</div>
Overwatering is the #1 cause of succulent death. These plants are designed to survive drought.
</div>

**The Soak and Dry Method:**

<div class="procedure">
<div class="step">
<strong>Check the soil</strong> - Insert your finger 1-2 inches deep. If it's dry, it's time to water.
</div>

<div class="step">
<strong>Water thoroughly</strong> - Soak the soil completely until water drains from the bottom.
</div>

<div class="step">
<strong>Let it dry completely</strong> - Wait until the soil is bone dry before watering again (usually 1-2 weeks).
</div>

<div class="step">
<strong>Adjust for seasons</strong> - Water less in winter when plants are dormant.
</div>
</div>

## Soil and Potting

### The Right Soil Mix

Succulents need **well-draining soil** that doesn't hold moisture. A standard potting mix will kill them.

<div class="tip">
<div class="tip-title">DIY Soil Mix</div>
Mix 2 parts potting soil, 1 part coarse sand, and 1 part perlite or pumice for perfect drainage.
</div>

### Choosing the Right Container

<div class="procedure">
<div class="step">
<strong>Drainage holes are essential</strong> - Never use containers without drainage
</div>

<div class="step">
<strong>Size matters</strong> - Choose a pot only slightly larger than the root ball
</div>

<div class="step">
<strong>Material choice</strong> - Terracotta breathes better than plastic, helping soil dry faster
</div>

## Common Problems and Solutions

### Problem: Leaves Turning Yellow or Translucent

**Cause:** Overwatering  
**Solution:** Stop watering immediately. Remove the plant from wet soil, let roots dry, and repot in fresh, dry soil.

![Image](https://pixabay.com/get/g44ecdee15abcb509aabbb0161ff42258aa3a0e73f85d08e967c895cdfe1bd10f7dce29d94232d9e0007c59a00c75c8d8cd2315ab89d3508b96e0ad6a04b36af8_1280.jpg)

<div class="image-caption">Signs of overwatering - yellow, mushy leaves</div>

### Problem: Leaves Shrinking or Wrinkling

**Cause:** Underwatering  
**Solution:** Give your plant a thorough soak. The leaves should plump up within a few days.

### Problem: Stretching or "Leggy" Growth

**Cause:** Insufficient light  
**Solution:** Move to a brighter location. Unfortunately, stretched growth won't reverse, but new growth will be compact.

![Image](https://pixabay.com/get/g8ae036be116de116050f0bef655b789f6c4564c8d724a61da8f738ca4515eb8036fdc7530126ebecaa1e40865d1dad1426e83688a07883b7a13ba7202c40f1bd_1280.jpg)

<div class="image-caption">Etiolation (stretching) due to low light</div>

### Problem: Brown Spots or Scorching

**Cause:** Too much direct sunlight  
**Solution:** Move to a location with filtered or indirect light, especially during peak sun hours.

## Propagation: Growing Your Collection

One of the joys of succulents is how easily they propagate. You can grow new plants from leaves, cuttings, or offsets.

<div class="procedure">
<div class="step">
<strong>Choose healthy leaves</strong> - Gently twist leaves from the stem, ensuring the base is intact
</div>

<div class="step">
<strong>Let them callus</strong> - Place leaves on dry soil and wait 2-3 days for the cut end to dry
</div>

<div class="step">
<strong>Mist occasionally</strong> - Lightly mist the soil (not the leaves) every few days
</div>

<div class="step">
<strong>Wait for roots</strong> - In 2-4 weeks, you'll see tiny roots and a new plant forming
</div>
</div>

![Image](https://pixabay.com/get/gc3a40f046a3374525fd071de6cb324320a25676b2d211b86fbd0277108847146b32f58a80110fe61c302a2c732cbfa6993654b5d39f8853dec9dc878311d8393_1280.jpg)

<div class="image-caption">Succulent propagation in progress</div>

<div class="tip">
<div class="tip-title">Pro Tip</div>
The best time to propagate is during the growing season (spring and summer) when plants are actively growing.
</div>

## Seasonal Care Guide

### Spring (Growing Season)
- Increase watering frequency
- Begin fertilizing monthly with diluted succulent fertilizer
- Repot if needed
- Move outdoors if desired (acclimate gradually)

### Summer
- Protect from intense midday sun
- Water more frequently in extreme heat
- Watch for pests (mealybugs, aphids)

### Fall
- Reduce watering as growth slows
- Stop fertilizing
- Prepare for dormancy

### Winter (Dormant Season)
- Water sparingly (once a month or less)
- Keep away from cold drafts
- Provide bright light to prevent stretching

## Quick Reference: Succulent Care Checklist

- [ ] Bright, indirect light (4-6 hours daily)
- [ ] Well-draining soil mix
- [ ] Container with drainage holes
- [ ] Water only when soil is completely dry
- [ ] Soak thoroughly, then let dry completely
- [ ] Rotate plants weekly for even growth
- [ ] Check for pests monthly
- [ ] Fertilize during growing season only
- [ ] Protect from extreme temperatures
- [ ] Enjoy watching them thrive!
